Overview

Public services are authorized, funded, organized, contracted, operated, maintained, and changed. Public Mechanics studies the machinery between a policy and the moment a person meets its result, across cities, counties, states, public authorities, utilities, schools, and libraries.

Most of that machinery stays out of view. The processes, rules, routines, and repairs that hold a service together are the ones people rely on and rarely notice. Students learn to trace how a service holds together, diagram it, and explain it to someone who has never seen inside it. By the end of the term, each student produces a deep inquiry into one public system, showing how invisible work, coordination, and repair keep it running.

Three frames

Three frames arrive in the first three weeks and carry through the rest of the term.

01The five lenses

Purpose
What is supposed to happen, for whom, and toward what outcome?
Authority
Who has the legal or institutional power to make it happen?
Capacity
Who has the people, knowledge, equipment, and organization to do the work?
Control
Who controls funding, vendors, information, assets, and major decisions?
Delivery
How is the work performed, monitored, maintained, changed, and evaluated?

02Civil Economics

Va = Vp + Ve + Vr

  • Va the total value an activity generates.
  • Vp the value captured privately, as profit, wages, fees, and equity.
  • Ve the value pushed onto people outside the transaction.
  • Vr the residual obligation left with the public after the activity ends.

03The ambiguity economy loop

  1. A regulated domain exists.
  2. A reframe detaches the activity from the category.
  3. Intermediaries operationalize the gap.
  4. Capital accelerates coordination faster than oversight.
  5. The costs land on public institutions.

The final project

Each student takes one public system and asks how it works. Three things are required: an interview with someone who operates the system, direct observation at the point of service, and a trace from statutory authority down to the shop floor. The result is a ten slide deck and a two page executive summary.

What we read

Readings attach to the mechanism they illuminate and arrive when it does.

  • Seeing Like a State, James C. Scott. Read against the five lenses and how the state makes a service legible.
  • The Utopia of Rules, David Graeber. Read against authorization, procedure, and discretion.
  • The Fifth Risk, Michael Lewis. Read against capacity and what a government quietly maintains.
  • Bonds of Inequality, Destin Jenkins. Read against money, debt, and who municipal finance serves.
  • Crabgrass Frontier, Kenneth T. Jackson. Read against special districts and the shape of local governance.
  • The Color of Law, Richard Rothstein. Read against residual obligation and how public systems produced exclusion.
  • The Color of Money, Mehrsa Baradaran. Read against value capture and who it reaches.
