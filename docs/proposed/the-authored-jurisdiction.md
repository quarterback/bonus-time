# A city with an answer key

**Format:** essay, speculative design project, and workshop format
**Inquiry:** method
**Worked out:** late July 2026

## The argument

No real city has a counterfactual. You can never say an eligibility process cost forty hours of applicant time and didn't have to, because there is nothing to compare it to. A fully written fictional jurisdiction, announced as fictional and detailed enough that people use it anyway, is the control group nobody can otherwise build.

The legacy modernization premise is wrong in a specific way. The problem isn't that the data is too valuable to move; data migrates. The problem is that the old system is the only surviving statement of the policy. Thirty years of amendments, waivers, consent decrees and settlements were encoded as procedure and written down nowhere else. "Too complex to replace" usually means nobody alive can say what it does.

So the fictional jurisdiction has to include its sediment: the 1987 consent decree, the waiver never unwound, the amendment that contradicts an earlier one, resolved in code by a programmer who retired. Because the intent was written down too, you can measure exactly where implementation drifted from it, which is what service archaeology can only infer in the real world.

## Workshop form

One denial as the unit: a notice, the rule it cites, the guidance memo that reinterprets the rule, the amendment behind the memo, the decree behind the amendment. Fifteen to twenty five documents. Groups answer "why was this person denied, and who decided," disagree, then see the written intent nobody could have reached from the record. The record does not contain the decision.

## Builds on

Service archaeology, Public Mechanics, live action service design, Cap Buffet's approach of testing rules before adoption.

## Literature to engage

James Q. Wilson, *Bureaucracy* (1989), for agency types to vary; Michel Crozier, *The Bureaucratic Phenomenon* (1964), on power held by whoever controls remaining uncertainty; Alvin Gouldner on rules nobody enforces.
