# The ledger can't see the person

**Format:** working paper or long essay
**Inquiry:** Digital statecraft and public capability
**Worked out:** late July 2026

## The argument

"We don't have the capacity to track it" is usually true and almost always the wrong diagnosis. The tracking that would answer the public's question was never possible with the data these systems produce, however many staff were added.

1. **Two systems, two owners.** Public finance accounts by fund and object code. It answers whether money was spent on an allowable thing by an authorized person, and it answers that well. It cannot answer what happened to the person. That lives in a provider's case management system, with a different owner, identity scheme and privacy rules. Nobody joins the two unless a court makes them. The Los Angeles cost per sheltered person existed only because a judge forced that join.
2. **Pass through multiplies it.** Federal to state to county to continuum of care to nonprofit to subcontractor. Each hop reports whatever its grant agreement said.
3. **The annual audit tests the wrong thing.** The single audit under Uniform Guidance tests whether costs were allowable. It is a compliance instrument that everyone treats as a performance instrument. A program can pass it for a decade while producing nothing, without breaking a rule.
4. **The contract never said what success was.** Most human services contracts specify effort ("operate 40 beds"), not results ("house 40 people for a year"). An audit finding that outcomes weren't tracked is downstream of a procurement that never defined one, and that is deliberate, because a measurable contract is a losable one.

**What AI changed, narrowly:** reading got cheap. Pulling four thousand subrecipient agreements and sorting effort clauses from outcome clauses was impossible at any price and is now a weekend. AI recovers nothing nobody recorded, and the binding constraint is legal access to records.

**Who pays for the truth:** nobody buys waste detection, because the finding embarrasses the buyer. The parties with standing are the ones who eat the cost without having caused it: budget offices, legislative fiscal offices, counties in county administered states.
