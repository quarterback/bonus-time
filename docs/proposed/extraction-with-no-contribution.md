# A null transaction

**Format:** essay
**Inquiry:** Markets, platforms and value capture
**Worked out:** June 2026, from a reading of Roland Meyer's "platform realism"

## The argument

Platforms graft onto the ordinary fabric of civic life: people posting to stay connected, sharing art that means something to them, keeping a forum alive. The platform extracts attention and data from that behavior and contributes nothing back. This is harsher than unequal exchange. There is nothing on the other side of the transaction, so the platform isn't a bad deal. It's a null one, and that claim removes the platform's alibi.

What follows is calcification rather than destruction. Society's connective tissue hardens into a form owned and filtered by a narrow class and reachable only by rental, while keeping enough apparent function to defeat the complaint that anything was destroyed.

Two supporting fragments from the same session:

- **Platform realism requires a platform.** Meyer's term depends on platforms as infrastructure: upstream aggregation, automated feedback loops and the counterfeiting of civic trust. Plain aesthetic sameness among human makers predates platforms and doesn't need them.
- **The bracketing move is the ideology.** Writing that sets platform economics aside to discuss cognition or aesthetics performs the alibi it claims to be neutral about.
