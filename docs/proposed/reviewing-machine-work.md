# You can't review what you never did

**Format:** essay or workshop session
**Inquiry:** Authority, judgment and public life
**Worked out:** late July 2026, from a report on KPMG's intern training

## The argument

Judgment used to be a byproduct of volume. An auditor learned what a normal revenue contract looks like by testing four hundred of them; spotting the odd one is what that experience feels like from the inside. Automate the four hundred and the reps disappear. An offsite or a boot camp cannot replace them.

Firms now want new hires to review AI output before they have ever produced the work. That inverts the dependency. A reviewer who has never done the task can't evaluate a plausible looking artifact, and plausibility is exactly what the generator is built to produce.

What follows for training: small forcing functions inside the real work beat intensive events. PwC's prompt asking employees how they plan to review an agent's work, generated at the moment of delegation, does more than a two hour exercise with an answer key. A puzzle with a solution trains puzzle solving; judgment is what you need when there is no solution and you still have to sign. And a session on the generator's characteristic failure modes belongs before any session on review technique.

## Builds on

Decision receipts (a record made at the moment of delegation), the AAR work, live action service design.
