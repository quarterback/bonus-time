# A corps for digital statecraft

**Format:** working paper, a follow up to the classification paper
**Inquiry:** Digital statecraft and public capability
**Worked out:** late July 2026, cut from the main paper on purpose

## The argument

If digital statecraft is a field, it needs people formed for it, and the classification paper shows the multilateral apparatus has professionalized documents instead of people. This paper proposes the form those people could take: a deployable corps assembled from parts that already exist in US law.

- **Deployment authority** from the US Public Health Service Commissioned Corps. Roughly 6,000 officers in eleven professional categories hold ordinary jobs across agencies and can be deployed by the Secretary's authority regardless of position, with a Ready Reserve that can mobilize in five days.
- **Formation** from the Foreign Service Institute: a standing school every member passes through repeatedly, which is what makes foreign service officers legible to each other across postings.
- **Rank logic** from the warrant officer: appointed from the field on demonstrated craft, carrying technical authority rather than command.
- **Tours rather than careers**, so the corps keeps circulating people back into practice.
- **Funding by mandate rather than fee**, which is the half of the GDS and 18F comparison that worked.

The register is closer to an expeditionary medic or a hostage negotiator than to a diplomat: formed by cases, sent into situations already failing, judged on whether the situation resolved.

## What a referee already pushed on

- **Democratic legitimacy.** A corps proposed on ability alone repeats the technocratic leviathan worry. The answer drafted: created by statute, funded by appropriation, reporting to the legislature, independent of client agencies but never of the constitutional chain.
- **The duty question.** A PHS officer serves the Secretary and still owes a clinician's duty to the patient. The digital officer's equivalent duty runs to the person subject to the system: the accuracy, contestability and legibility of the determinations made about them. That duty is what separates a corps from a vendor.
- **Scope.** A national model built for replication, on the foreign service pattern, where the multilateral layer is national corps that can recognize each other. Not a world institution.
- **Cautionary precedent.** The Army Corps of Engineers, a technical corps that became a construction agency.
