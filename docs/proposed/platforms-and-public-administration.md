# No unit for platforms

**Format:** essay, possibly the anchor talk for the masthead Research section
**Inquiry:** Markets, platforms and value capture
**Worked out:** July to November 2026

Fuller working notes on this argument were written into a repo on 1 November 2026 under the title "Public Mechanics: the argument, the throughline and the literature." This brief points to the same material.

## The argument

Public administration doesn't have the tools to run a city once platforms sit between the city and its people. It is organized by function: budgets, departments, procurement, sector regulation. A platform is not a vendor, a utility or a constituent. It touches housing, transportation, small business licensing and labor at once, so no office owns it. Public Mechanics responds to that blind spot by studying the mechanisms that cut across departments and reach the person, which is where platforms now stand.

Public administration measures its own process. Public delivery measures what reaches the person.

## Strength of each claim

- **Public administration can't see platforms.** Strongest ground.
- **Platformization drew down social trust.** Defensible in the form already written in "the rails of trust": platforms moved into an existing gap and took over the institutions that used to rebuild trust. Not defensible as the cause of the decline, which started decades earlier.
- **Social media as the underrated villain of current politics.** Weakest to lead with. Works as a consequence of the first two.

- Rachel Botsman as the foil: trust moved from institutions to platforms. The answer is that a review is private, can't be contested and can be withdrawn at will; a license or a public record could be contested.
