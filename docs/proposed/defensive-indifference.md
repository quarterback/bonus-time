# Defensive indifference

**Format:** essay; a coined term anchored to cases
**Inquiry:** Digital statecraft and public capability
**Worked out:** July 2026

## The argument

In baseball, when the defense doesn't contest a runner taking a base, the scorer records defensive indifference. The runner gets no stolen base, and the official record keeps why the base was taken.

The state does the same thing without the scorer. It declines to contest a private system's advance into space the state built (licensing, public records, local institutions), and the advance is then treated as natural rather than as a policy choice. Each concession looks like reasonable triage on its own. Added up, it is an uncontested defense. And unlike baseball, nobody keeps the record of why, so the state also loses its memory of having chosen.

Two related gaps from the same session:

1. **Substitution, not deregulation.** Public trust instruments such as licensing regimes and contestable public records get swapped for private optimization systems, which moves the record of judgment out of public reach.
2. **Lost institutional memory.** The contestable public record of who decided and on what basis disappears. This connects directly to the provenance and decision receipt work.

## Rule for the piece

The term only works if every use of it is pinned to a documented case. Coinage and evidence arrive together or the term doesn't get used.

## Literature to engage

Bo Rothstein, Onora O'Neill, Julie E. Cohen. The neo Brandeisian antitrust work (Matt Stoller, Lina Khan) is adjacent; this argument differs by focusing on the state's withdrawal from producing trust.

## Needs

Three cases where a city or state stopped contesting a platform's entry and the entry was later described as inevitable.
