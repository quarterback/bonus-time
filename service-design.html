Overview

Service design covers the visible and invisible actions that make up everyday life. In a city, the interactions between people and places, through transactions, actions, and rituals, are how we get our days, weeks, and years to work. Service design is a way to understand those experiences and a practical tool for changing them.

The first half of the term builds the core skills through cases, methods workshops, and field observation, including a day at Detroit Metro Airport, where students read an entire airport as a service. The second half puts those skills on a live project with Michigan’s state courts, where the class works as a civic design studio to reimagine the legal experience for the people who move through it.

By the end

By the end of the term, students will be able to:

  • Run mixed methods research, ethnographic and desk, to understand a service context in depth.
  • Map a service in full, reading the frontstage a person meets and the backstage that staff operate, and locate the pain points and opportunities in the journey.
  • Build presentations that tie every claim to a research insight, and pitch them to the people in the room.
  • Hold user experience and operational reality together in the design artifacts you make.
  • Turn insights into a working digital prototype that shows an improved service experience.

The live project

For most people, access to justice looks like a PDF. A confusingly worded checkbox, a cramped text field, a sheet of instructions that expects a law degree. In the second half of the term, the class works with Michigan’s state courts on the forms that decide whether a person can halt an eviction, reclaim a security deposit, or file a claim without a lawyer.

01How it runs

Part one is shared research: form audits, field observation at court facilities, service and journey mapping, synthesis, and critique. Part two is individual work. Each student picks one part of the court experience and builds a digital prototype that shows the current state and the reimagined one beside it.

02The midterm

Pairs take two documents from Michigan’s small claims suite: the instructions for the Affidavit and Claim, and the sheet on collecting a judgment. Teams work in an agile rhythm, a kanban board moving cards from to do to in progress to done, a cycle of research to prototype to documentation, and a running log of every change with the reason behind it.
