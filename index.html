I go where the hard problems are.

People usually reach out because they want a straight answer from someone who can see the shape of a problem, ask the right questions, and stay close enough to delivery to know what will actually work.

Straight answers.

My work spans an array of responsibilities beyond the traditional areas where a designer shows up.

I have operated from front-end code, content, research, and service design through statewide digital operations, cross-agency programs, executive advising, and multidisciplinary organizations of more than 40 people.

That range is useful when the easy categories stop helping. Whether the problem is a consequential project that needs rescuing, a mission-critical resource a community needs now, or a team that needs structure and room to grow, I know how to get oriented, find the constraint, and build a credible plan of attack.

Leadership without distance.

My dad always told me that if you are going to be in charge, you need to know how to mop the floors too.

That is a metaphor. You cannot afford my floor-mopping rate.

I have led teams, big and small, for a long time. I have hired and coached managers, built practices, allocated people across portfolios, and helped teams work hard, ship, and grow people for their next jobs. I still write, research, prototype, facilitate, teach, debug, and make things. Leadership is part of my practice. Staying useful is the point.

How services actually work.

I keep coming back to the mechanics behind a service: who has authority, where discretion lives, how paperwork moves, which handoffs fail, what the technology assumes, and who absorbs the friction.

Those details decide whether a policy becomes something people can use or another burden they have to survive.

Making a broken system more efficient is not progress.

I look at trajectory. What is this system becoming? What happens downstream? Where will harm or administrative burden accumulate? What can change the outcome before the wrong thing hardens into infrastructure?

A wider field of view.

My reference points are deliberately broad. I have worked a few blocks from the White House and near towns where you could smell cattle and sugar beets in the air. I spend part of my summers in Finland, learning from Nordic and European leaders who take public delivery seriously and put words into action.

I also coach, teach, write, build software, and launch things outside formal roles. Operating inside and outside technology, government delivery, and design keeps me closer to real people's problems and gives me more ways to understand what is actually happening.

Where I am useful.

Call when a project is stuck, a mandate is larger than the team, an institution has an idea but no path to delivery, or a technology conversation has outrun operational reality.

I can help frame the problem, test whether the organization is ready, shape a service or program, build or repair delivery capability, assemble the right people, and tell you when the responsible answer is to stop, shrink, or go somewhere else.

The job I want rarely has a neat description. The useful mandate gives me room to connect strategy, people, technology, operations, and consequences, then stay involved long enough to see whether the thing actually works.

Phone a friend.

Maybe you need advice, not an upsell. Maybe you have a delivery problem to tackle and want another view from someone who has been there. Send me a note.
